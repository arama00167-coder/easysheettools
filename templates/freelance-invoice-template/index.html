What's inside this freelance invoice template

  • Hourly billing built in: hours × your rate, calculated automatically
  • Project/task rows for fixed-price work too
  • Payment details section: bank transfer, PayPal or Wise
  • Invoice number and due date for polite, firm follow-ups
  • Clean design clients trust — no logo needed to look pro

How to use it — step by step

  1. Add your name/business name and contact details at the top.
  2. Enter the client's name and the invoice date and number.
  3. List your work: task, hours and your hourly rate (or a fixed amount).
  4. Add your payment details so the client can pay in one step.
  5. Send as PDF and note the due date in your Client & Project Tracker.

The formula, explained simply

Amount = Hours × Hourly Rate

Each row multiplies hours by rate (or takes a fixed project fee), and SUM builds the invoice total. The sample invoice totals 840 for 14 hours of design and development work.

A worked example

8 hours of web design at 45/hour (360) plus 6 hours of development at 80/hour (480) totals 840 — the worked example in the file, ready to copy.

Frequently asked questions

What should a freelance invoice include?

Your name, the client's name, an invoice number, the date, a clear list of work, the total, payment details and a due date. This template has all of them.

How do I charge late-paying clients?

Send the invoice as a PDF on the day work is delivered, and follow up politely 3 days before the due date. A 'late fee' note line is included if you want it.

Can I invoice in another currency?

Yes — amounts are plain numbers. Invoice in USD, EUR, GBP or any currency your client pays in.
