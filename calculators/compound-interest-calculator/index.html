Compound Interest Calculator

The most motivating calculator on this site: a starting amount plus a steady monthly deposit, growing year after year. Watch interest start earning its own interest — and see exactly what you deposit versus what growth adds.

Last updated: October 31, 2026 · ✓ Formula checked · Free, no sign-up

Your savings plan

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your starting amount (can be 0).
  2. Add your monthly deposit — even a small one counts.
  3. Set the expected yearly growth rate and the number of years.
  4. Read the future value and the year-by-year table.

The formula behind it

FV = P(1+r)ⁿ + PMT × ((1+r)ⁿ − 1) ÷ r

Applied monthly: r is the yearly rate ÷ 12 and n is years × 12. The first part grows your starting amount; the second grows the stream of monthly deposits.

Worked example

Starting with 1,000 and adding 200 a month at 7% yearly growth for 10 years builds about 38,700 — you deposited 25,000 and growth added roughly 13,700.

Frequently asked questions

What growth rate should I use?

For a savings account, use its actual interest rate. For long-term index investing, 7–8% is a common planning assumption — but returns are never guaranteed.

Why do monthly deposits matter so much?

Because every deposit starts compounding from the month it lands. Starting five years earlier usually beats depositing more later.
